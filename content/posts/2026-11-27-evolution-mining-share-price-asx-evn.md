Evolution Mining is one of the largest gold producers listed on the Australian Securities Exchange, trading under the code EVN. If you are checking the Evolution Mining share price, it helps to know what the company owns and which numbers the market reacts to. This guide covers both. It does not tell you whether to buy or sell.

For a live quote, use the ASX website or your broker. Prices move through the day, so we don't quote one here.

## What Evolution Mining owns

Evolution runs six mines, according to its FY26 disclosures:

- **Cowal** in New South Wales, wholly owned
- **Northparkes** in New South Wales, 80% owned
- **Ernest Henry** in Queensland, wholly owned
- **Mt Rawdon** in Queensland, wholly owned
- **Mungari** in Western Australia, wholly owned
- **Red Lake** in Ontario, Canada, wholly owned

Gold is the main product, but copper matters more than many investors realise. Ernest Henry and Northparkes are copper-gold mines, and copper sales are counted as a by-product credit that lowers Evolution's reported cost per ounce of gold.

## The FY26 numbers

Evolution's financial year ends on 30 June. In FY26 it produced 714,728 ounces of gold and 65,636 tonnes of copper, according to its annual financial report. Both were lower than FY25, when it produced about 751,000 ounces and 76,000 tonnes. A weather event at Ernest Henry in December 2025 cost roughly 12,000 ounces of gold and 8,000 tonnes of copper.

Profits still hit records, because prices rose faster than output fell. Underlying EBITDA was A$3,171 million, up from A$2,207 million, and statutory profit after tax was A$1,475 million. All-in sustaining cost (AISC) was A$1,717 an ounce, up from A$1,572.

> **The number:** A$1,717. That was Evolution's all-in sustaining cost per ounce in FY26. The gap between that figure and the Australian dollar gold price is roughly the company's margin on every ounce.

## What FY27 guidance says

For FY27 the company guided to 660,000 to 730,000 ounces of gold and 63,000 to 70,000 tonnes of copper, at an AISC of A$1,795 to A$1,995 an ounce. Production is expected to be weighted to the second half of the year as new mining areas ramp up.

Growth work is under way at three sites: the open pit continuation at Cowal, the E22 development at Northparkes and the Bert development at Ernest Henry.

## What moves Evolution Mining's shares

- **The gold price in Australian dollars.** Evolution sells in US dollars but reports in Australian dollars, so both the metal and the currency matter.
- **The copper price,** through the by-product credit. A fall in copper raises Evolution's reported gold cost even if nothing changes at the mines.
- **Quarterly delivery against guidance.** Evolution reports production each quarter. A weak quarter early in a second-half-weighted year can still leave the full year on track, but markets often react first.
- **Costs.** FY27 guidance points to higher costs per ounce than FY26. Coming in at the low or high end of that range is worth watching.
- **Dividends and capital spending.** Record cash flow funded a higher dividend in FY26, according to the results call. How the company splits cash between dividends, growth projects and debt is a recurring question for shareholders.

## Risks to keep in mind

Gold prices can fall as quickly as they rose. Weather has already disrupted Ernest Henry once. Growth projects can run late or over budget, and higher guided costs leave less room for error if prices soften.

## What we're watching

- **The December-quarter production report,** and whether output is tracking towards the FY27 range.
- **Copper prices,** which [have fallen sharply in recent weeks](/briefings/copper-price-slump-ai-tariff-trades-unwind) and feed straight into Evolution's cost per ounce.
- **Progress at Cowal, Northparkes and Ernest Henry,** the projects that underpin future production.
