Read any mining company announcement and you will meet words like "Inferred Mineral Resource" or "Probable Reserve". They are not marketing language. They are defined terms, set by industry bodies and enforced by securities regulators, and they tell you how much confidence to place in the numbers.

In Canada those definitions come from the Canadian Institute of Mining, Metallurgy and Petroleum (CIM). In Australia they come from the JORC Code. This guide explains both, how they compare, and why it matters when you read about a junior explorer.

## What the Canadian Institute of Mining does here

The Canadian Institute of Mining, Metallurgy and Petroleum is a professional body for the minerals industry. Among other things, it publishes the **CIM Definition Standards for Mineral Resources and Mineral Reserves**, most recently updated in May 2014. These set out what counts as a resource or a reserve, and the categories within each.

The CIM standards become legally binding through **National Instrument 43-101**, the Canadian securities rule for disclosure about mineral projects. NI 43-101 adopts the CIM definitions, so any company listed in Canada that uses the words "resource" or "reserve" must use them as CIM defines them.

## What NI 43-101 adds

NI 43-101 is a disclosure rule, not just a set of definitions. Among other things, it requires:

- **A Qualified Person,** an experienced and professionally registered geoscientist or engineer, to prepare or approve the scientific and technical information.
- **Technical reports** in a set format for material projects, filed publicly.
- **Clear warnings** when a company talks about economic studies based on lower-confidence resources.

## The JORC Code in Australia

The JORC Code, the Australasian Code for Reporting of Exploration Results, Mineral Resources and Ore Reserves, plays the same role for companies listed on the ASX. The ASX Listing Rules require mining companies to report under it. Instead of a Qualified Person, JORC requires a **Competent Person** to take responsibility for the numbers.

## How the categories compare

The two systems use almost the same ladder, because both follow an international template:

- **Inferred Mineral Resource:** the lowest confidence. Enough sampling to suggest a deposit exists, but not enough to plan a mine on.
- **Indicated Mineral Resource:** more drilling and testing, enough to support mine planning.
- **Measured Mineral Resource:** the highest confidence in the geology, grade and continuity.
- **Probable and Proven Reserves:** the parts of Indicated and Measured resources that can be mined economically, once mining, processing, economic and legal factors are applied. JORC calls these Ore Reserves, CIM calls them Mineral Reserves.

> **The rule of thumb:** a resource is what is in the ground; a reserve is what a company has shown it can mine at a profit. Inferred resources are the least certain of all.

Because the definitions match so closely, NI 43-101 accepts reports prepared under JORC and some other foreign codes, and dual-listed companies can usually translate between them. Differences remain in how much technical detail each requires, with NI 43-101 generally more prescriptive about disclosure.

## Why this matters for juniors

Small explorers often talk about historical work: old mine records, past production or grades reported decades ago. Under both systems these are **historical estimates**, not current resources. NI 43-101 lets a company mention them only with clear caveats, including that a Qualified Person has not done enough work to classify them as current.

That is why our coverage of companies such as [Krait Critical Minerals and its Bales antimony project](/briefings/bales-antimony-1950s-stockpile-krait-critical-minerals) stresses that old grades are "not a current resource". The first modern drilling programme is what turns history into a defined resource, or doesn't.

## What to check in an announcement

- **Which category** the numbers are in, and how much is Inferred.
- **Who signed off:** the Qualified Person or Competent Person named.
- **Whether there is a technical report** or full JORC table behind the headline.
- **Cut-off grade and price assumptions,** which can make a resource look bigger or smaller.
