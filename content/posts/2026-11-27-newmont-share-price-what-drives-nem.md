Newmont is the world's largest gold producer by output, and one of the few big miners whose shares trade on both the New York Stock Exchange and the Australian Securities Exchange. Many people still search for the "Newmont Mining share price" or "Newmont Mining Corporation". The company dropped "Mining" from its name years ago and is now Newmont Corporation, but the code is still NEM.

This guide explains where Newmont trades, what it owns, what its latest results showed and what tends to move the shares. It is general information, not a recommendation. For a live price, check the NYSE or ASX quote through your broker.

## Where Newmont trades

Newmont's main listing is on the New York Stock Exchange, priced in US dollars. Since late 2023, after it bought Australia's Newcrest Mining, it has also traded on the ASX as CHESS Depositary Interests (CDIs), also under the code NEM. Each CDI represents one Newmont share, so the ASX price tracks the New York price converted into Australian dollars. Newmont applied in September 2025 to delist from the Toronto Stock Exchange.

## What Newmont owns

The Newcrest deal gave Newmont a large Australian and Pacific business, including the Cadia copper-gold mine in New South Wales and Lihir in Papua New Guinea. These sit alongside Boddington in Western Australia, Tanami in the Northern Territory and mines across the Americas and Africa. Newmont has since sold a number of smaller mines to focus on its largest, longest-life operations. It also produces meaningful amounts of copper and silver.

## What the third quarter showed

In the third quarter of 2026 Newmont produced about 1.3 million attributable ounces of gold, plus 11 million ounces of silver and 23,000 tonnes of copper, according to its results release. It said it is on track for full-year output of 5.3 million attributable ounces, with fourth-quarter production expected to rise to about 1.4 million ounces.

Costs fell. All-in sustaining cost was US$1,454 an ounce in the quarter, and full-year AISC guidance was lowered to about US$1,520 an ounce, roughly 10% below where it started the year. With an average realised gold price of US$4,317 an ounce, the quarter produced US$2.8 billion of operating cash flow and US$1.9 billion of free cash flow.

> **The number:** almost US$2,900. That is roughly the gap between Newmont's realised gold price and its all-in sustaining cost per ounce in the third quarter, which is why cash flow hit a record.

The company declared a quarterly dividend of US$0.26 a share and had US$2.8 billion left under its US$6 billion buyback programme. Management describes 2026 as a production low point, with output expected to grow modestly from 2027.

## What moves Newmont's shares

- **The gold price.** Newmont is a large, liquid way for funds to own gold exposure, so its shares often move with bullion and with expectations for US interest rates and the dollar.
- **Costs and guidance.** Falling costs this year helped. Any reversal, or a miss on production guidance, tends to hit the shares quickly.
- **Copper.** Cadia and other mines make Newmont a sizeable copper producer, so copper prices feed into its costs through by-product credits.
- **Big projects.** The Red Chris block cave in Canada and other long-life developments carry cost and timing risk that the market watches closely.
- **Capital returns.** Dividends and buybacks are a large part of the investment case for many holders.
- **The Australian dollar,** for anyone holding the ASX-listed CDIs. A stronger Australian dollar can lower the ASX price even if the New York price is unchanged.

## What we're watching

- **Fourth-quarter output** and whether Newmont hits the 5.3 million ounce target.
- **2027 guidance,** due early next year, and how much of the promised growth shows up.
- **Gold and copper prices,** after a volatile few weeks, especially for [copper](/briefings/copper-price-slump-ai-tariff-trades-unwind).
