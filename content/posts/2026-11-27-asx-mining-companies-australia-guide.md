Australia is one of the world's biggest mining countries, and the Australian Securities Exchange (ASX) is home to hundreds of listed mining firms. They range from global giants to explorers with a single drill rig. For investors, the most useful first step is to sort them by what stage they are at, because each stage is moved by different things.

This guide explains how the ASX mining sector is grouped, what drives each group, and the questions worth asking before looking at any one company. It is general information, not a recommendation.

## The three kinds of mining company

**Producers** dig, process and sell metal today. They have revenue, costs and, often, dividends. The diversified majors such as BHP and Rio Tinto sit here, as do iron ore producer Fortescue and mid-sized gold miners like [Evolution Mining](/briefings/evolution-mining-share-price-asx-evn). So does Newmont, the US gold major, whose shares also trade on the ASX after it bought Newcrest in 2023.

**Developers** have found a deposit, studied it and are raising money to build a mine. Their value depends on getting finance, permits and construction done on time and on budget.

**Explorers**, often called juniors, are still looking. They usually have no revenue, spend their cash on drilling and come back to the market for more. Their share prices react sharply to drill results.

> **The rule of thumb:** the earlier the stage, the more a company's value rests on things that haven't happened yet, and the wider the range of outcomes.

## What moves each group

- **Commodity prices.** A producer's profit moves with the price of what it sells. Because most Australian miners report in Australian dollars but sell in US dollars, a weaker Australian dollar can lift their earnings even when the metal price is flat.
- **Costs.** For gold miners the key cost measure is all-in sustaining cost (AISC) per ounce. The gap between AISC and the gold price is roughly the margin.
- **Guidance.** Producers publish yearly production and cost guidance. Missing it, even narrowly, often moves the share price more than the metal price does.
- **Financing.** Developers and explorers rely on raising money. Placements are usually priced at a discount and add new shares, which dilutes existing holders.
- **Jurisdiction.** Where the mine is matters. Australian-listed companies run mines in Australia, Africa, Asia and the Americas, and a change in a host country's rules can matter as much as geology. [Kingsgate Consolidated](/briefings/kingsgate-share-price-asx-kcn-chatree), whose only mine is in Thailand, is a good example.

## The main commodity groups on the ASX

- **Iron ore**, Australia's largest mining export, dominated by the majors.
- **Gold**, the busiest group by number of listed companies, from large producers to hundreds of explorers.
- **Copper**, increasingly sought after for electrification, and often produced alongside gold.
- **Lithium and battery metals**, a boom-and-bust group tied to electric vehicle demand. Foreign producers are involved too: Chile's [SQM](/briefings/sqm-lithium-chile-mt-holland-explained) co-owns the Mt Holland lithium mine in Western Australia.
- **Critical minerals** such as rare earths and antimony, where government policy in the US and Australia is a big driver.

## How to read an ASX mining announcement

ASX-listed mining companies must report exploration results, mineral resources and ore reserves under the JORC Code. That is why announcements talk about "Inferred", "Indicated" and "Measured" resources, and why each one names a Competent Person who takes responsibility for the numbers. Our [guide to JORC and NI 43-101](/briefings/jorc-vs-ni-43-101-cim-definition-standards) explains the categories and how they compare with the Canadian rules.

Explorers and developers also lodge a quarterly activities report and cash flow report. The cash figure and the expected spend for the next quarter tell you roughly how long a company can go before it needs to raise money again.

## Where investors look for opportunities

Investors tend to approach the sector in one of a few ways:

- **Producers with growing output or falling costs,** where results can be checked each quarter.
- **Developers close to a funding decision,** where the main risks are finance and construction.
- **Explorers in proven districts,** where a discovery can re-rate a company quickly, but most drilling does not lead to a mine.
- **Commodity exposure,** choosing a company mainly for the metal it produces.

None of these is a shortcut. Mining shares are volatile, small companies can lose most of their value, and commodity cycles can turn quickly.

- **Gold producers' December-quarter reports** in January, and whether production and cost guidance are holding.
- **Lithium prices,** which have weakened again, according to producers such as SQM, and set the tone for every battery metals stock.
- **Critical minerals policy** in Canberra and Washington, which shapes funding for rare earths, antimony and other strategic metals.
