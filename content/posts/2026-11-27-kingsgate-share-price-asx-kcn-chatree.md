Kingsgate Consolidated is an ASX-listed gold and silver producer, trading under the code KCN. People searching for the Kingsgate mining share price are really asking about one asset: the Chatree gold mine in central Thailand. Almost everything that moves the shares runs through that mine and the country it sits in.

This guide covers Kingsgate's history, its latest production numbers and the main things to watch. It is general information, not a recommendation. For a live price, check the ASX quote through your broker.

## A mine that closed and came back

Chatree was Thailand's largest gold mine when the Thai government ordered it shut in 2016, and production stopped that December. In 2017 Kingsgate began arbitration against Thailand under the Thailand-Australia Free Trade Agreement, arguing the closure was an unlawful expropriation.

Over the next few years the two sides moved towards a settlement. Thailand granted new mining leases, and Chatree restarted in 2023, more than six years after it closed. The restart was opposed by some local civil groups. In November 2025 Kingsgate and Thailand agreed to end the arbitration without an award, and the termination took effect in January 2026, closing a dispute that had run for almost eight years.

## What Chatree is producing now

Kingsgate's financial year ends on 30 June. In FY26 Chatree produced 86,078 ounces of gold, up 15% on the year before, and 766,009 ounces of silver, according to the company's production report as covered by Kalkine. On a gold-equivalent basis that was 97,159 ounces. The company's original gold guidance was 85,000 to 95,000 ounces.

The June 2026 quarter produced 20,163 ounces of gold and 220,078 ounces of silver, the sixth quarter in a row above 20,000 ounces. Kingsgate was added to the S&P/ASX 200 index around the same time, according to The Motley Fool Australia.

> **The number:** 20,000 ounces. That is roughly the quarterly gold output Chatree has held for six straight quarters, and the baseline the market now measures each report against.

## Why the shares can fall on good news

Higher production does not guarantee a higher share price. Capital Brief reported that Kingsgate's shares dropped sharply after the FY26 numbers, even though output rose. With a single mine, expectations get built into the price quickly, and any detail that falls short, such as costs, grade or the outlook, can outweigh the headline.

In July 2026 Kingsgate also reported an interruption at Chatree's Plant 1 processing facility, with several updates that month. For a one-mine company, even a short stoppage matters.

## What moves Kingsgate's shares

- **Gold and silver prices.** Chatree produces both, so both matter.
- **Quarterly production and costs,** measured against guidance and the 20,000-ounce run rate.
- **Plant reliability.** Processing problems at Chatree hit the whole company.
- **Thailand.** Kingsgate depends on a good relationship with the Thai government and local communities. The end of the arbitration removed one uncertainty, but political and permitting risk remains.
- **Index inclusion.** Being in the ASX 200 brings in index funds, but also means the shares can move with large index rebalancing trades.

## What we're watching

- **The next quarterly production reports,** and whether Chatree stays above 20,000 ounces a quarter.
- **FY27 guidance** and the cost outlook.
- **Any change in Thai mining policy** or community relations around Chatree.
